This result must not yet be converted into a calibration scale factor. A single hand-moved trajectory is insufficient to separate persistent scale bias from run-to-run effects such as motion profile, residual attitude change, manual endpoint placement, scene geometry, and frontend behavior.

The non-zero attitude change is an important confounder for evaluating scale and the full camera↔IMU transform. It should be retained rather than normalized away.

## Interpretation

This run establishes that:

- the live pipeline survives a real 500 mm hand-moved trajectory;
- the output displacement is of the correct order and direction magnitude;
- current single-run metric scale is approximately `0.911` relative to the 500 mm ground-truth distance;
- the absolute distance error is much larger than the stationary baseline, so it represents motion/scale behavior rather than simple stand-still noise;
- a repeatability series is required before modifying calibration or applying any empirical scale correction.

## Required next step

Repeat the same 500 mm protocol several times with the same endpoints. Prefer at least three valid runs. Preserve each individual scale/error result. Do not tune intrinsics, extrinsics or timestamps between runs.

The purpose is to determine whether the approximately 9% under-scale is reproducible or mainly run-dependent. If repeated scales cluster tightly, investigate systematic causes. If they vary strongly with hand motion or speed, characterize motion-profile dependence before any calibration change.
